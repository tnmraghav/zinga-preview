The short answer

Second charge, explained in plain English.

Written by the Zinga Financial Services team
Updated 26 September 2026

A second charge mortgage is a separate loan secured on your home, alongside your existing mortgage. It may be considered if remortgaging would mean losing a good rate or paying a large early repayment charge. It is secured borrowing, so we always compare it with alternatives such as a further advance or remortgage first.

Who this is for: Homeowners who want to raise money — for example for home improvements — without changing their existing mortgage.

Your options

Your adviser compares these with you and explains the trade-offs before you decide.

01

Further advance

Borrowing more from your current lender.

02

Remortgage

Replacing your mortgage with a larger one.

03

Second charge

A separate secured loan, keeping your current mortgage in place.

Before you start

Costs and risks to know about.

Costs to budget for

  • Lender fees
  • Valuation and legal fees
  • Our fee, if any — explained in writing first

Risks

Think carefully before securing other debts against your home. Your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments on your mortgage or any other debt secured on it.

We explain the risks of any recommendation in writing before you proceed.

FAQ

Questions we're often asked.

Still unsure? Call an adviser.

01Is a second charge the same as a secured loan?

Yes, the terms are often used for the same thing.

02Will it affect my current mortgage?

Your existing mortgage stays in place, but both loans are secured on your home.

03When is it not a good idea?

If a further advance or remortgage would cost less overall, or if the repayments would stretch your budget.
