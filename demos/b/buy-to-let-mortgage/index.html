I'm a landlord

The short answer

Buy-to-let mortgages, in plain English.

Buy-to-let lenders mainly assess whether the expected rent covers the mortgage payment by a set margin, known as a rental stress test. Most require a deposit of at least 25%. Criteria differ for first-time landlords, limited company purchases and portfolio landlords, which is where advice helps.

Who it's for

New and experienced landlords buying or remortgaging a rental property, personally or through a limited company.

What will it cost, and what are the risks?

Costs and risks to know about.

Costs to budget for

  • Deposit — usually 25% or more
  • Stamp Duty including the additional-property surcharge
  • Lender fees, legal fees and valuation
  • Our fee, if any — explained in writing first

Please read

Your property may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments on your mortgage. Most buy-to-let mortgages are not regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.

Buy-to-let questions.

Didn't find your answer? Ask us directly.

How much deposit do I need?

Usually at least 25%, though some lenders need more depending on the property and rent.

Can I be a first-time landlord?

Yes, some lenders accept first-time landlords, often with extra criteria such as owning your own home.

Should I buy through a limited company?

It depends on your tax position. We explain the mortgage side and recommend speaking to an accountant.
